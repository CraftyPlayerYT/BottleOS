# 📋 BottleOS — Contribution Rules

Welcome to BottleOS! These rules exist so everyone knows what to expect.
By submitting **any** contribution (pull request, issue, patch, or otherwise),
you agree to all the rules below.

---

## 1. 🚀 How to Contribute

1. Fork the repository and create a branch for your changes.
2. Pick a task from the project todolist or open an issue to propose one.
3. Write your code following the standards in section 4.
4. Test your work — it must build and run correctly.
5. Open a pull request describing **what** you changed and **why**.
6. Your code will be reviewed before being merged.

---

## 2. 📜 License & Code Ownership

- BottleOS is licensed under the **GNU General Public License v3.0**.
- By contributing, you agree that your contribution is released under the **GPL v3**.
- You remain credited as the author of your work in the contributors list.
- BottleOS is built on top of the **Linux kernel** and other free software;
  their licenses are respected and credited.
- Do **not** submit code you do not have the right to share. Respect the
  licenses of any third-party code or assets.

> ⚠️ Because of the GPL v3, no contribution can be made proprietary.
> Everything stays open source.

---

## 3. 💝 Funding & Editions

- The **Student**, **Individual**, and **AI** editions are free.
- The **Enterprise** edition is paid, and **100% of its revenue is donated
  to charity** — BottleOS is driven by passion, not profit.
- Contributors are credited for their work. Any contributor reward program,
  if introduced, will be announced transparently with clear rules known
  in advance.

---

## 4. ✅ Coding Standards

- Write clear, readable code. Comment anything non-obvious.
- Keep contributions focused: one feature or fix per pull request.
- Match the structure of the project (`kernel/`, `student/`, `ai/`, etc.).
- Test before submitting — broken code will not be merged.
- Document new features in the `docs/` folder when relevant.

> ⚠️ Contributions that do not follow these standards may be rejected.

---

## 5. 🤝 Community & Conduct

- Be respectful. No insults, harassment, or discrimination.
- Disagreements are welcome and normal — discuss them openly and politely.
- If a decision seems unfair to you, you are free to raise it. Feedback
  makes the project better.
- The maintainers (including the project creator) make final decisions,
  but commit to explaining the reasoning behind them.

---

## 6. 🛠️ Issues & Bug Reports

- Search existing issues before opening a new one.
- Describe the problem clearly: what happened, what you expected, how to
  reproduce it.
- Include your system info when relevant (edition, version, hardware).

---

## 7. 📝 Final Notes

- These rules may evolve. Any important change will be announced.
- When in doubt, ask before submitting — questions are always welcome.

---

*BottleOS — Open source, open future.* 🍾
